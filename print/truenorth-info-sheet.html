The first ninety days

What the next ninety days actually look like

Nobody hands you a brochure and wishes you luck. Here is the real shape of it.

1

You reach out

A phone call, or the form on our website. We answer, we ask a few questions, and we tell you straight whether we have a bed and whether we’re the right fit. If we’re not, we’ll tell you who is.

2

You move in

Usually within 48 hours. You get a bed, a key, a drawer, and a house that already knows your name. First week you’re just finding the coffee.

3

You find footing

Weekly house meeting, daily check-in, a curfew that loosens as you earn it. We help you get to work, to meetings, to court if that’s where you need to be.

4

You go the distance

Most people stay six to nine months. Some become house leaders. Some move out and come back for Sunday dinner. The door doesn’t close behind you.

Why we started

True North is a direction, not a destination.

Recovery is about more than staying sober — it’s about building a meaningful life. We founded TrueNorth Living to provide more than just a place to stay: a home where people find structure, accountability, community, and the support needed to confidently move forward.

“You don’t arrive at it. You just keep checking whether you’re still pointed the right way — and it helps to have people around who’ll tell you when you’re not.”

How we run a house

A house, not a facility.

Small on purpose

Six to ten residents per house. Big enough for a real community, small enough that your absence is noticed the same day.

Structure you can feel

Curfew, chores, house meetings and random testing. Not to punish — because the shape of a day is the thing most of us lost first.

Peer-led, not managed

House leaders are residents who’ve been where you are. Staff support the house; the house runs itself. That’s the part that sticks.

Honest about money

One clear rate per house, posted publicly. No intake fee, no surprise charges. If you hit a rough patch, come talk to us before you disappear.

Dignity is not a reward. You don’t earn respect by staying sober long enough. It’s the starting condition, and it’s not revocable.
We say the hard thing early. If a house isn’t working for someone, we have that conversation while there’s still time to fix it — not at the point of exit.
Recovery outlasts residency. Alumni still come back for Sunday dinner. The relationship doesn’t end when the rent does.
Cost & what you get

What it costs, in plain numbers.

Rates are per person and set per house — some are charged weekly, some every two weeks, some monthly.

We’ll post the exact rate the moment our first house opens. Call in the meantime and we’ll tell you what we’re planning — including what’s included and what isn’t.

Insurance, honestly. Sober living is housing, not clinical treatment, so health insurance usually does not pay for it directly. Be skeptical of any operator who tells you otherwise. What insurance can cover is the clinical care you receive while living here — and we’ll help you line that up.

What you get

What’s included, and what isn’t.

What’s included
  • Your bed, furnished — shared or private, depending on the house
  • All utilities — water, electric, gas, internet
  • Household supplies and cleaning products
  • Drug and alcohol testing
  • House meetings and peer support
  • Help getting to work, meetings and appointments
What’s not
  • Groceries and personal food
  • Personal toiletries
  • Phone, transportation and personal expenses
  • Clinical treatment, therapy or medication — we help you connect to these, we don’t provide them

Straight answers

Questions people actually ask

How fast can I move in?

Often within 48 hours if a bed is open. If not, we’ll tell you where you sit on the waitlist and give you a real estimate — not a maybe.

Do I have to be detoxed first?

Yes. Sober living is not detox. You need to be medically stable and past withdrawal. If you’re not there yet, call anyway and we’ll help you find a program.

How long do people stay?

Most stay six to nine months. There’s no maximum — longer stays predict better outcomes, so we won’t push you out on a schedule.

Do you accept MAT?

Yes, case by case. Medication must be legally prescribed, taken as directed, and managed to house policy.

Are you a men’s or women’s house?

Both, in separate houses. TrueNorth does not operate co-ed residences.

Is my information private?

Yes. We don’t confirm anyone lives here without their written permission — including to family.
